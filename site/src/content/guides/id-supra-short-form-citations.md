Short-form citations save space, but they also create some of the most context-dependent citation questions in a document. The text `Id. at 42` cannot be evaluated safely without knowing what authority came before it and whether another source intervened.

## Why short forms are different

A full citation usually contains enough information to identify an authority. A short form relies on information elsewhere in the document. That means review requires more than pattern matching.

The reviewer may need to know:

- the nearest preceding authority;
- whether the prior citation contained multiple authorities;
- whether another authority intervened;
- whether the citation appears in body text or a footnote;
- whether a `supra note` reference points to one source or several;
- whether a hereinafter label was actually defined.

## Id.

`Id.` generally refers to the immediately preceding authority when the context permits it. A mechanical checker can normalize capitalization or punctuation in a citation-shaped use, but resolving the antecedent requires document context.

Common review questions include:

- Does the preceding citation contain one authority or multiple authorities?
- Has a new authority appeared between the full citation and `Id.`?
- Is the pinpoint format appropriate for the source?
- Does the use cross a paragraph or note boundary that changes the relationship?

Ordinary prose containing the word "id" must not be converted into a citation. A safe system therefore uses both citation shape and document relationships.

## Supra

`Supra` usually points back to an earlier authority that is eligible for that short form. The author name, title fragment, note number, or hereinafter label helps identify the source.

A reviewer should confirm:

1. the referenced source appeared earlier;
2. the short form identifies it unambiguously;
3. the source category permits the short form;
4. the pinpoint or note reference is correct;
5. no journal or local rule changes the required form.

## Supra note

A `supra note` citation can be especially ambiguous when the referenced footnote contains more than one authority. A checker should not simply choose the first source in that note.

## Shortened case names

A shortened case citation may use a distinctive party name plus reporter and page information. The shortened name should still identify the intended case clearly, and the reporter or pinpoint should match the full citation.

Document-wide review helps prevent a shortened name from being linked to the wrong case when similar party names appear.

## Statutory and regulatory short forms

Sections, subsections, and subsequent references may rely on a previously identified code or regulation title. A reviewer should confirm that the document has established the necessary context and that the shortened reference cannot be confused with another authority.

## Hereinafter

A hereinafter label works only when it was defined clearly and used consistently. Review the original definition, quotation marks or typography, and every later occurrence.

## A conservative automation rule

A system should resolve a short form only when the document provides one defensible relationship. When multiple candidates remain, the correct automated result is **ambiguity**, not a confident guess.
